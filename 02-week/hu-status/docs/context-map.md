# Context Map — Distributed Reservation Platform

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial bounded contexts and the relationships between them for the Distributed Reservation Platform.

The Context Map is used to establish clear domain boundaries before implementing the microservices.

The initial architecture is based on business capabilities, not technical layers.

The goal is to avoid unnecessary service fragmentation and to ensure that each possible microservice has a clear responsibility and owns its own data.

## 2. Bounded Contexts

The initial domain analysis identifies the following bounded contexts:

| Bounded Context           | Main responsibility                                                        | Data ownership                                           |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity and Access       | Manage user identity, authentication, and authorization.                   | Users, credentials, roles, and permissions               |
| Space Management          | Manage reservable spaces and their availability.                           | Spaces, availability rules, and blocked periods          |
| Reservation Management    | Manage the reservation lifecycle.                                          | Reservations and reservation states                      |
| Payment Management        | Manage payments associated with reservations.                              | Payments, payment states, and transactions               |
| Notification Management   | Manage notifications generated by relevant business events.                | Notifications, delivery status, and preferences          |

## 3. Identity and Access context

### Responsibility

Identity and Access is responsible for managing identity and access rules for users who interact with the platform.

Its main responsibilities are:

- Register users.
- Authenticate users.
- Authorize operations.
- Manage roles.
- Manage permissions.
- Validate access to resources.

### Data ownership

The context owns:

- Users.
- Credentials.
- Roles.
- Permissions.

Other services must not access this data directly.

### Main interactions

Identity and Access provides identity information and authorization checks to the other services when needed.

## 4. Space Management context

### Responsibility

Space Management is responsible for administering the physical spaces that can be reserved and the rules related to their availability.

Its responsibilities are:

- Register spaces.
- Update space information.
- Manage availability.
- Define unavailable periods.
- Validate whether a space can be requested for a given period.

### Data ownership

The context owns:

- Spaces.
- Availability rules.
- Blocked periods.

### Main interactions

Reservation Management queries Space Management when it needs to validate the availability of a space.

## 5. Reservation Management context

### Responsibility

Reservation Management is responsible for the reservation lifecycle.

Its responsibilities are:

- Create reservations.
- Validate reservation conditions.
- Maintain reservation states.
- Confirm reservations.
- Cancel reservations.
- Publish reservation-related events.

### Data ownership

The context owns:

- Reservations.
- Reservation states.
- Information related to the reservation lifecycle.

### Main interactions

Reservation Management communicates with:

- Identity and Access to identify and authorize users.
- Space Management to validate availability.
- Payment Management to coordinate payment.
- Notification Management through reservation events.

Reservation Management is the central business context of the initial platform because it coordinates the main reservation flow.

## 6. Payment Management context

### Responsibility

Payment Management is responsible for administering payments associated with reservations.

Its responsibilities are:

- Create payment operations.
- Query the status of a payment.
- Process payment results.
- Avoid duplicate financial effects.
- Publish payment-related events.

### Data ownership

The context owns:

- Payments.
- Payment states.
- Transaction identifiers.
- Information related to payment processing.

### Main interactions

Payment Management receives payment requests associated with reservations.

After processing it may publish events such as:

- PaymentConfirmed.
- PaymentFailed.
- PaymentCancelled.

Reservation Management consumes these events to update the reservation lifecycle.

## 7. Notification Management context

### Responsibility

Notification Management is responsible for delivering notifications generated by relevant business events.

Its responsibilities are:

- Receive notification events.
- Create notification records.
- Deliver notifications.
- Query delivery status.
- Handle delivery failures.

### Data ownership

The context owns:

- Notifications.
- Delivery status.
- Notification preferences.

### Main interactions

Notification Management consumes events such as:

- ReservationCreated.
- ReservationConfirmed.
- ReservationCancelled.
- PaymentFailed.

Notification delivery is not part of the main reservation transaction.

Therefore, a notification failure must not invalidate a reservation that has already been confirmed.

## 8. Relationships between contexts

The initial relationships between bounded contexts are:

| Source context          | Target context            | Relationship                                      | Communication |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Identity and Access     | Reservation Management    | Provides identity and authorization information   | Synchronous   |
| Space Management        | Reservation Management    | Provides availability information                 | Synchronous   |
| Reservation Management  | Payment Management        | Requests payment processing for a reservation     | Synchronous   |
| Payment Management      | Reservation Management    | Publishes the payment result                      | Asynchronous  |
| Reservation Management  | Notification Management   | Publishes reservation-related events              | Asynchronous  |
| Payment Management      | Notification Management   | Publishes payment-related events                  | Asynchronous  |

## 10. Communication strategy

The architecture will use synchronous and asynchronous communication.

### Synchronous communication

Synchronous communication will be used when the requesting context needs an immediate response.

Initial examples:

- Reservation Management queries the availability of a space.
- Reservation Management requests payment creation.
- Services validate identity or authorization information when needed.

The initial synchronous communication will use REST API contracts.

### Asynchronous communication

Asynchronous communication will be used when the sender does not need an immediate response and the process can be decoupled.

The initial events include:

- PaymentConfirmed.
- PaymentFailed.
- ReservationCreated.
- ReservationConfirmed.
- ReservationCancelled.

The specific messaging mechanism will be selected during implementation and detailed architectural design.

## 11. Data ownership

Each bounded context owns its business data.

The initial ownership is:

| Bounded Context           | Own data                                                 |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity and Access       | Users, credentials, roles, and permissions               |
| Space Management          | Spaces, availability rules, and blocked periods          |
| Reservation Management    | Reservations and reservation states                      |
| Payment Management        | Payments and payment states                              |
| Notification Management   | Notifications and delivery status                        |

No bounded context may read or modify another bounded context's database directly.

Information between contexts must be obtained through explicit APIs or asynchronous events.

## 12. Consistency strategy

The platform will use different consistency models depending on the business operation.

### Strong consistency

Strong consistency, or an equivalent coordination mechanism, is required to:

- Create a reservation.
- Validate availability.
- Confirm a reservation.
- Apply critical changes to a payment state.

The goal is to avoid:

- Duplicate reservations.
- Duplicate financial effects.
- Invalid reservation states.

### Eventual consistency

Eventual consistency is acceptable for:

- Notifications.
- Analytics.
- Reports.
- Non-critical read models.

For example, a reservation may be confirmed before the Notification Service finishes delivering the confirmation notification.

## 13. Failure isolation

The bounded contexts are designed to reduce the impact of failures between services.

For example:

- A notification failure must not cancel a confirmed reservation.
- A temporary Notification Service outage must not prevent Reservation Management from keeping the reservation state.
- Payment failures must be represented explicitly through payment events.
- Duplicate events must be processed idempotently when necessary.

This separation allows individual capabilities to evolve and fail independently.

## 14. Initial microservice candidates

The initial bounded contexts are candidates to become independent microservices:

1. Identity Service.
2. Space Service.
3. Reservation Service.
4. Payment Service.
5. Notification Service.

The definitive implementation boundaries will be confirmed after reviewing the domain model and architectural decisions.

The project will not add extra microservices only to increase the number of services.

A new service must have:

- A significant business responsibility.
- A clear domain boundary.
- Independent data ownership.
- A defined communication contract.
- A justified need for independent scalability or deployment.

## 15. Future extensibility

The architecture will allow new bounded contexts to be added when justified.

Some possible future contexts are:

- Reviews.
- Promotions.
- Search.
- Loyalty.
- Analytics.
- Audit.

These capabilities are outside the initial MVP.

Their future addition must not require direct access to the databases of existing services.

## 16. Architectural principles

The Context Map follows these principles:

1. Business capabilities define service boundaries.
2. Each service owns its data.
3. Services communicate through explicit contracts.
4. Databases are not shared between services.
5. Synchronous communication is used when an immediate response is needed.
6. Asynchronous communication is used when processes can be decoupled.
7. Critical operations must consider idempotence.
8. Failures of non-critical services must not invalidate completed business transactions.
9. New services must have a justified boundary.
10. The number of services must stay proportional to the real needs of the project.

## 17. Initial conclusion

The initial domain analysis identifies five bounded contexts that are suitable candidates to become independent microservices.

Reservation Management is the central business capability of the initial platform, coordinating availability, reservations, payments, and reservation-related events.

The Context Map establishes the initial boundaries and communication relationships used as input for the architectural decision documented in [`adr-001-architecture.md`](./adr-001-architecture.md).

The architecture may be refined during implementation as new knowledge about the domain is obtained.
